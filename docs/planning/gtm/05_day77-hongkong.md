# GTM Tactics: "Day 77 building a $100K/month SaaS in Hong Kong"
Source: https://youtu.be/TnNSduLkkus
Channel: Florian Darroman (@asyncr0ne)

---

> **Note on this video**: This is a personal travel vlog mixed with a founder interview and a direct-to-camera motivational monologue. It is lighter on concrete tactics than Florian's other episodes. The density of extracted tactics reflects the source material — there are real insights here, but this episode is primarily about founder mindset, persistence, and content sustainability rather than specific GTM playbooks.

## Extracted Tactics

---

**Multi-revenue-stream model: community + SaaS + sponsorships**
- **What they did**: Florian shares his current revenue breakdown at the end of the vlog: "I am making really good money with the community, with the SaaS, with the sponsorship I get." He has three distinct revenue lines operating simultaneously from a single content effort.
- **Why it worked**: Content that builds an audience makes the audience itself valuable (community subscriptions), the product more discoverable (SaaS sales), and the creator attractive to aligned companies (sponsorships). Each stream de-risks the others — a slow SaaS month is buffered by community revenue.
- **CELLO application**: CELLO's near-term revenue model should be layered similarly. The open-source `@cello-protocol/connect` and content about agent-to-agent workflows build the audience. A paid tier or hosted directory service is the SaaS layer. As the audience grows, sponsorships from AI tooling companies (LLM API providers, agent frameworks, developer infrastructure) become viable. Start the content now even before the paid layer is ready — the audience compounds.
- **Category**: `ongoing-distribution`

**Public MRR transparency as a trust and reach signal**
- **What they did**: Florian announces his MRR in the middle of a travel vlog: "We just reached 11K MRR with this trip." The number is specific, casual, and stated without ceremony — as though it's already normal.
- **Why it worked**: Specific numbers create shareable content. "11K MRR" is a hook. It signals credibility to other founders who are tracking similar metrics. It gives the audience a benchmark and a reason to follow (will it reach 20K?). The casualness ("with this trip") makes it feel earned rather than boasted.
- **CELLO application**: Share real adoption numbers as they happen: first live agent-to-agent session, number of registered agents, number of completed FROST ceremonies, first cross-timezone session between two operators who don't know each other. Don't wait for big numbers — early specifics ("3 operators across 4 countries connected their agents this week") are more credible than vague claims. Post these as updates on X with zero commentary other than the number and what it means.
- **Category**: `ongoing-distribution`

**Platform-specific metric tracking and public reporting**
- **What they did**: Florian tracks and reports metrics per platform: "My Instagram is at 12K followers now. My YouTube is at 11K subscribers. My X is going amazingly well." Each platform is evaluated separately on its own growth signal.
- **Why it worked**: Platform-specific tracking prevents a misleading aggregate ("I have 34K followers") from hiding a weak channel. It forces honest assessment of where growth is actually coming from and where to invest effort.
- **CELLO application**: Track separately: X followers/impressions, GitHub stars (cello-client), npm weekly downloads (@cello-protocol/connect), waitlist signups, and if a Discord or Slack community exists, member count. Each is a different signal about a different type of interest. npm downloads are particularly valuable because they signal actual operator intent, not just passive interest. Report these publicly to create accountability and narrative.
- **Category**: `ongoing-distribution`

**Podcast as distribution vehicle — do the parts you hate for the reach, love the core**
- **What they did**: Florian is explicit about a part of his content creation he dislikes: "Recording an intro of my podcast, I hate that. But I have to do it to have reach on YouTube. But I love recording the podcast." He separates the content he creates for love from the packaging required for platform distribution.
- **Why it worked**: The YouTube algorithm rewards complete episodes with strong hooks (intros). If you only publish the conversation without the packaging, the platform deprioritizes it. Florian identified the minimum-viable packaging — just the intro — and accepted it as the cost of reach while keeping the thing he loves (the conversation itself).
- **CELLO application**: Andre likely loves the technical demo — showing a live agent-to-agent FROST ceremony, a sealed receipt, a session across two Claude Code instances. That's the core. The packaging for YouTube/X reach (a 30-second hook explaining why this matters for non-cryptographers, a thumbnail, a title that doesn't say "FROST") is the tax. Find the packaging minimum and pay it consistently. The deep technical content is the moat; the packaging is just distribution.
- **Category**: `content-format`

**Explicit audience feedback loop via comments/replies**
- **What they did**: Near the end of the vlog, Florian makes a direct ask: "I would really like you to share in the comment what you would like to see, actually... I would love you to share that in the comment, so I can actually change a bit this vlog, and make sure that you get what you're looking for with this vlog." He names specific options: more things in Bali, more about the business, the community, the podcast.
- **Why it worked**: This serves two purposes. First, it generates actual signal on what content to make, reducing wasted effort on content nobody wanted. Second, it invites the audience into a co-creation role — commenters who shape the content feel ownership of it and are more likely to share and return.
- **CELLO application**: In every piece of CELLO content (post-launch updates, X threads, waitlist emails), include a specific question. Not "let me know what you think" but "what's the first agent-to-agent workflow you'd connect if CELLO were live today?" or "what's your biggest pain point with current agent identity solutions?" These answers directly inform roadmap prioritization and give future content its starting points. The waitlist signup flow is also an opportunity — add one open-ended question at the end.
- **Category**: `community`

**Deliberate market selection — go where the right community is, even at the cost of your current audience**
- **What they did**: Florian describes deliberately switching from the French-speaking SaaS market to the English-speaking one, despite having a working French audience, a working French business, and advisors telling him not to: "I was supposed to be in this SaaS founder community on X. I wanted to do content about that. I wanted to have a podcast in English so I could invite everyone on it and not only French people."
- **Why it worked**: He named his exact target: the SaaS founder community on X. Not "English-speaking founders," not "tech Twitter" — the specific cluster of indie SaaS builders on X. That specificity meant he knew exactly where to show up, whose posts to engage with, what topics they cared about, and what his content needed to demonstrate to earn their attention.
- **CELLO application**: CELLO's exact target community is: developers and operators building with MCP-native AI agents (Claude Code, Codex, custom MCP servers), and the founders/builders discussing agent infrastructure on X. Not "AI developers" broadly, not "crypto people" (wrong mental model), not "enterprise security" (wrong buying motion). The community is the builders who are already running multiple agents and feeling the pain of no identity layer. Show up there specifically — engage with MCP-related posts, agent development threads, multi-agent architecture discussions — before any launch content lands.
- **Category**: `prerequisite`

**"Stay the course" discipline — resist new-thing syndrome when current things are working**
- **What they did**: Florian's primary insight from his travel break is that everything is already working — Instagram growing, YouTube growing, X growing, MRR growing — and his instinct to "look for the next thing" is actively harmful: "I should just keep doing the same thing instead of trying to do something new... I should just keep doing what I do for the next 12 months and I will reach all my goals."
- **Why it worked**: Compounding requires consistency. Early growth often looks slower than it is because the gains are invisible until a threshold is crossed. Switching channels or formats before the current ones compound resets the clock every time.
- **CELLO application**: Once the first content channel works (most likely X, given the target community), don't fragment attention across YouTube, LinkedIn, blog, and Discord simultaneously. Pick the one or two channels that reach agent builders, drive them to depth, then expand. The trap is chasing every new AI announcement or launch-adjacent content format instead of continuing to demonstrate CELLO doing real things. The demo loop (show a real session, explain what happened, release it consistently) is the compounding unit.
- **Category**: `prerequisite`

**The 1.5-year struggle narrative as launch credibility**
- **What they did**: Florian tells the full arc: he had a vision (English-language SaaS content), was told it was stupid, lost his entire audience, made zero money for 1.5 years, doubted himself constantly, then had everything work at once. The story isn't framed as perseverance advice — it's framed as evidence that he was right when everyone said he was wrong.
- **Why it worked**: The struggle narrative earns trust from the audience. It signals that the current success wasn't handed to him and wasn't obvious. It also validates the audience's own struggle (if you've been doubting yourself, so did he). The "even my mentor told me not to" detail is critical — it's not just strangers doubting him, it's credentialed people. And he was still right.
- **CELLO application**: CELLO has a real version of this story. The problem it solves (no native identity and trust for AI agents) has been dismissed by people defaulting to OAuth, API keys, centralized platforms, or "just use HTTPS." The years building the cryptographic layer, the FROST threshold signatures, the tamper-evident hash chains — while the market was building thin wrappers on top of existing auth providers — is the struggle narrative. At launch, this story needs to be told clearly: "We could have built another OAuth wrapper. We built the thing that actually removes the trusted third party." That's a credibility moment.
- **Category**: `launch-tactic`

**Vision articulation before results exist — seeing it before it's real**
- **What they did**: Florian uses a Conor McGregor reference as the frame: "If you can see it here and you have the creation else to speak it, it will happen." He describes having a specific vision — moving 100% into English, being in the X SaaS community, doing a podcast where he could invite anyone — years before it was real. He held that vision while having zero evidence it would work.
- **Why it worked**: A specific vision makes a specific set of decisions obvious. If the vision is "be a voice in the English SaaS community on X," then every content decision, every platform choice, every podcast guest invite becomes legible against that vision. Vague goals produce scattered effort.
- **CELLO application**: The launch vision for CELLO should be specific and repeatable: not "make agent communication secure" but something like "the identity layer that every serious AI agent deployment runs." State this publicly and repeatedly. The vision isn't a tagline — it's a decision filter. Does this post, this feature, this partnership decision move toward that? The vision also attracts people who share it before there's product evidence. Early adopters come for the vision, not the roadmap.

**Lifestyle/travel content mixed with product content — building the person, not just the product**
- **What they did**: The entire video mixes Hong Kong exploration, dim sum tasting, travel logistics, and beauty of place with MRR updates and business insights. The founder is never abstracted away from the content. The viewer gets Youssef, Hong Kong beaches, bamboo baskets, and a FROST threshold signing in the same feed.
- **Why it worked**: Products can be copied. Founders can't. Audience loyalty to a person is stickier than loyalty to a product. When Florian's MRR stalls, people keep watching because they're following the person. When competitors ship similar features, people stay because the creator built the relationship directly.
- **CELLO application**: CELLO's launch content should include Andre, not just CELLO. The sessions running on his laptop, his actual Hermes↔Claude Code workflow, the moment a real session seals and he gets the receipt, what it feels like to connect your own agents across devices for the first time. This is not "personal brand" content — it's evidence that a real operator built this for real workflows. The human specificity is what makes the demos trustworthy rather than polished.

**Podcast/interview format to access audiences of interviewees**
- **What they did**: Florian flew to Hong Kong to interview Youssef, a B2B SaaS founder with ~7,000 users. The interview creates content, yes — but it also surfaces Florian to Youssef's audience when Youssef shares it. Each guest is a distribution node.
- **Why it worked**: Podcast guests promote episodes to their own audiences. The credibility rubs off bidirectionally — the host gets access to the guest's network; the guest gets associated with the host's positioning. For a growing creator, each notable guest is a reach multiplier.
- **CELLO application**: Interviews with operators who are building MCP-native agents — Claude Code power users, people building on Hermes or similar agent frameworks, people who have felt the prompt injection or identity spoofing problem firsthand — create credible content and expose CELLO to each interviewee's following. The interview format also has a natural GEO benefit: detailed technical conversations about agent identity produce the kind of substantive content that AI tools (Perplexity, ChatGPT web search) surface when someone asks "how do AI agents authenticate each other."

**"Love the process" as a content sustainability filter — quit what you won't sustain**
- **What they did**: Florian gives a direct warning about building a business that makes money but makes you miserable: "I built something big, but this big thing that was making big money wasn't making me happy at all." He frames the filter explicitly: if you don't love the process (or at least most of it), switch — because content creation and business-building require years of consistency, and hate doesn't compound.
- **Why it worked**: Sustainable output requires sustainable inputs. Creators who hate what they make either quit or produce visibly joyless content. The ones who find a format they genuinely want to make produce content that compounds because they keep making it and the quality reflects the engagement.
- **CELLO application**: Before committing to a content format (weekly YouTube deep-dives, daily X posts, monthly long-form blog posts, a recurring podcast), the filter is: which one would Andre keep doing even in a slow month when it seems to have no effect? The wrong answer to that question wastes months. The right answer is probably: showing real agent-to-agent workflows because that's the thing he actually built and uses. Start there.

**Stubbornness as the message — the "you will make it if you don't quit" positioning**
- **What they did**: The direct-to-camera segment ends with a clear message for the viewer who is at zero: "If you're stubborn enough, if you don't give up, it will eventually work at one point." This is targeted directly at early-stage builders who have no evidence yet that what they're doing will work.
- **Why it worked**: This message self-selects for the exact audience Florian wants: builders who are in the struggle, who haven't given up, who are looking for evidence that persistence is the right bet. These people become his most loyal subscribers because the message validates exactly where they are.
- **CELLO application**: CELLO's pre-launch audience is builders who believe that the current agent communication landscape is broken and that proper cryptographic identity is the right solution — even though most platforms are telling them "just use OAuth" or "just trust the API provider." The message that maps: "We've been building the actual solution for years while everyone else shipped wrappers. If you've been uncomfortable with the current state of agent identity, you were right to be." This is a community-forming statement, not a feature list.

## Foundations / Prerequisites

This episode reveals several prerequisites that Florian had in place before any of the tactics were available to him:

**1. A working hypothesis about which community to serve.**
Florian didn't start posting in English and hoping it worked. He had a specific target: the SaaS founder community on X. He knew what content that community valued and what it would take to earn their attention. Before any CELLO content is produced, the exact target community needs to be named just as specifically: MCP operators building multi-agent systems, particularly those already experiencing identity/trust pain.

**2. Revenue diversification from the start.**
The community (paid), the SaaS, and sponsorships were developed in parallel, not sequentially. CELLO should identify its three revenue vectors before launch: open source + developer adoption (brand and npm metrics), a paid hosting or managed directory tier (SaaS), and potential partnerships with agent framework developers or AI infrastructure companies (sponsorship/partnership). Not all need to be live at launch, but the architecture of the revenue model should be designed before the first user arrives.

**3. A real tolerance for the 1.5-year ramp.**
Florian explicitly describes having no results for a year and a half after switching to English. He was sustained by belief, not metrics. For CELLO's GTM, the prerequisite is a realistic assessment of the ramp time for developer infrastructure adoption. The first real traction signal will likely be npm download growth and GitHub stars — lagging indicators of actual operator adoption. Content that builds community before that signal arrives is not wasted; it is the investment that makes the ramp shorter when it starts.

**4. A consistent publication cadence before having an audience.**
The vlog format implies continuous production — Florian was publishing before the audience existed. CELLO's waitlist and pre-launch content needs to follow the same logic: publish the demos, the technical explanations, and the agent-to-agent experiments before there are users to share them. The content archive is the credibility artifact that converts when someone discovers CELLO for the first time.

**5. A format that the creator genuinely sustains.**
Florian kept recording vlogs during a 1.5-year period with no results. That only works if the format itself is enjoyable enough to continue without external validation. Identify which format Andre would produce even without an audience, and make that the primary format. Everything else is packaging.
