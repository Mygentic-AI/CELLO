# GTM Tactics: "Day 1 growing my SaaS to $100K/month"
Source: https://youtu.be/OHRcn5rx85A
Channel: Florian Darroman (@asyncr0ne)

---

> **Context:** This is episode 1 of Florian's "building in public" series. He has just joined Borja's SaaS (Distribute.io, formerly Rebel Growth) as a co-founder, one week in. Borja is the tech/product founder who already validated the product to ~10K MRR using paid ads and growth hacks. Florian brings distribution, content creation, and SEO. The product itself is a GTM/distribution engine for busy founders — auto-generates SEO content, social posts, Reddit citations, and backlinks. They commit publicly to 10K → 200K MRR.

## Tactic Entries

---

**[Co-founder split: one builder, one distributor]**
- **What they did**: Borja (technical founder, product builder) explicitly sought a co-founder whose ONLY job is distribution and content — not to help with product. Florian's background is 10 years of SEO and affiliate, plus audience building. Borja handles all product, paid acquisition, and growth engineering. Florian handles all content creation and audience distribution. The division is hard and up-front.
- **Why they chose it**: "You can't do everything yourself." Borja tried the content game before and found it pulls focus away from product quality. With AI-saturated SaaS, product differentiation requires a dedicated product owner. Distribution in 2026 requires a dedicated distributor with a real audience and a face behind the brand. Brand-backed auto-content alone is not sufficient — there is a meaningful gap between it and content from a real human creator-face. Quote: "there's a difference" between branded content and content backed by an individual's reputation.
- **CELLO application**: Andre is solo. This tactic directly names the solo-founder trap: if you build AND distribute, both suffer. The question is whether CELLO has the audience or the open-source community gravity to substitute for Florian's role — or whether there is a co-founder or developer-advocate hire that should happen before launch to own the distribution half. At minimum, this framing clarifies: distribution is a full-time role, not a part-time add-on.
- **Category**: `prerequisite`

**[Build in public as a video series with episode numbers]**
- **What they did**: Framed the entire growth journey as an episodic building-in-public series, starting at "Day 1" with real numbers (76 paid users, ~10K MRR), a stated goal (200K MRR), and a consistent format: introduce the founders, show the product, share the numbers. The episode is published as a YouTube video with the product demo embedded in the narrative.
- **Why they chose it**: Two effects. First, radical number transparency creates accountability that attracts a committed audience — people root for the journey and return for updates. Second, the series format compounds: every new episode is content that can reference earlier episodes, creating internal linking and a reason to binge. Florian ran the same format on his podcast (interviewing successful founders), which gave him enough credibility to be sought out as a co-founder. Quote: "I will not lie to you, I started to get itchy" — the founder's personal stake is made visible, which is audience-building fuel.
- **CELLO application**: Andre can run a weekly/bi-weekly "building CELLO" series: start with the real numbers (waitlist count, npm installs, active agents in the network), name the target (e.g. "1,000 registered agents by end of Q3"), and document the specific growth moves each episode. The series is also a forcing function — committing publicly to a goal makes the work real. CELLO's open-source + cryptographic angle gives episodes unique angles: "This week I shipped threshold signing for agents" is not a story Distribute.io can tell.
- **Category**: `content-format`

**[Rebrand on co-founder join — name the new era]**
- **What they did**: The moment Florian joined, they rebranded from "Rebel Growth" to "Distribute.io". New name, new logo, new dashboard design, all in one week. The rebrand is documented and shown on camera — before/after screenshots in the video.
- **Why they chose it**: The old brand was "his" (Borja's). The new brand is "ours." Quote: "I wanted the tool to look like us and not look like him." The rebrand also sharpened the product's positioning — the name "Distribute" is direct about what the product does, which lowers CAC on cold traffic. Showing the rebrand in the video gives the audience a concrete "Day 1" artifact to anchor the series around.
- **CELLO application**: If CELLO's current branding was shaped by solo-founder defaults, a co-founder or designer join is the right moment to revisit it. Even without a co-founder, CELLO is at a similar inflection: moving from "protocol people" to "product people." The launch itself is the rebranding moment — the public-facing positioning should be re-evaluated before the first launch post, not after.
- **Category**: `prerequisite`

**[Use your own product as the primary showcase ("dogfooding on camera")]**
- **What they did**: Florian plugged Distribute into his own product (Open Cloud Lab, his school community) immediately upon joining. One week later, he had citations in Google AI Overview, ranked on ChatGPT, Grok, and other LLMs. He shows this on camera with live screenshots — "if you don't trust me, go on any LLM and ask for best Open Cloud community." The product demo is therefore not a slide deck or a sandbox; it is a live result on a real product.
- **Why they chose it**: Nothing closes a demo loop like a founder showing their own product generating measurable results for their own other product. It collapses the credibility gap instantly. It also generates social proof without any customer testimonials — the product sells itself through its own founder's behavior. Quote: "the few people who tried it see the value straight away."
- **CELLO application**: Andre uses CELLO daily (Hermes ↔ Claude Code, local ↔ AWS). This is already happening — the dogfooding is live. The missing step is making it visible. Every time CELLO enables a real multi-agent interaction Andre is doing for actual work, that is a demo moment: screenshot the sealed receipt, show the session transcript, show the LLM query that triggered the agent-to-agent handoff. The product demo should be Andre's real workflow, not a staged sandbox.

**[Validate to traction BEFORE seeking distribution help]**
- **What they did**: Borja explicitly notes that he brought Florian into a product that already had 76 paid users and ~10K MRR — validated with real money, not promises. Quote: "I validated the idea, and I'm coming to you with a product that's got traction." The co-founder search was not "help me find product-market fit"; it was "help me scale what already works."
- **Why they chose it**: Distribution amplification is expensive (time and money). Amplifying a product before product-market fit is burning resources. Borja's framing is also a negotiating point — he could bring a content co-founder in on favorable terms because he had proof.
- **CELLO application**: CELLO has a working protocol, registered agents in the network, open-source npm installs, and Andre using it daily. That is traction, even if not revenue. The question is whether it is enough to claim "validated" before the distribution push. The analog is: before investing in the GEO/SEO/content flywheel, can Andre point to 10-20 developers who have installed cello-client and connected agents? Those installs are the Borja-moment for CELLO.

**[Reddit citation engine as the LLM-ranking lever]**
- **What they did**: Built a "Reddit engine" into their product as a dedicated feature. Every day the system finds a relevant subreddit, a relevant thread or topic, and posts a natural comment that includes a brand mention. The explicit goal is LLM ranking — they claim Reddit citations are the primary signal driving ChatGPT/Claude/Grok/Gemini brand mentions. Quote: "This is what moves the needle for LLM ranking."
- **Why they chose it**: LLMs are trained on Reddit. A brand that appears naturally in Reddit discussions (not spammy, not promotional — "natural comment") gets picked up as a real-world signal of what the community says about the brand. This is a GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) strategy. The tactic is scalable because it is automated, and it targets the training data surface directly rather than waiting for organic reputation to accumulate.
- **CELLO application**: CELLO should be showing up in threads about: MCP servers, agent-to-agent communication, AI security, FROST/threshold signatures, Claude Code workflows, multi-agent orchestration, prompt injection defense. These are all active Reddit communities (r/LocalLLaMA, r/ClaudeAI, r/MachineLearning, r/ArtificialIntelligence, r/devops for AI). Florian's team automates this — for CELLO, the manual version is Andre (or a developer-advocate) participating authentically in those threads with real answers, where CELLO is the organic recommendation. The key is "natural comment" — not spam, but genuine engagement that incidentally mentions CELLO when relevant.
- **Category**: `seo-geo`

**[AI SEO content calendar: one article per day, one keyword per article]**
- **What they did**: Distribute auto-generates one SEO article per day per user, each targeting a single keyword that is "easy to rank for." Articles are published directly to the user's blog (WordPress, Shopify, Ghost, Framer, webhook). Each article is then repurposed into social media posts across all major platforms. The content calendar is auto-generated from the user's URL — zero human input after setup.
- **Why they chose it**: Consistent daily publishing compounds over time. Targeting easy-to-rank keywords (long-tail, low competition) builds a portfolio of ranked pages that bring in consistent organic traffic. The zero-input model lowers the activation energy so founders who "don't have time for content" actually ship it. Florian notes his own test: "These articles are starting to rank even if I just added this website on Distribute a week ago."
- **CELLO application**: CELLO's blog/docs site should have a programmatic SEO strategy. Long-tail keywords like "how to connect two Claude Code agents," "MCP server identity verification," "agent-to-agent authentication," "AI agent trust layer," "FROST threshold signature agents" are low-competition and directly map to CELLO's protocol. One high-quality technical article per week (not per day — quality matters more for technical audiences) targeting a specific developer question would compound over 6-12 months. The SEO strategy here should prioritize developer intent (how-to, what-is, comparison) over brand terms.
- **Category**: `seo-geo`

**[Content repurposing pipeline: one article → all social channels]**
- **What they did**: Every SEO article generated by Distribute is automatically repurposed into posts for LinkedIn, X (Twitter), Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest, YouTube, and Reddit. The entire social presence is derived from the blog content pipeline — no separate social content strategy.
- **Why they chose it**: Maximizes the ROI of each piece of content by distributing it across all surfaces where the target audience might find it. Different platforms have different algorithm dynamics (LinkedIn favors thought leadership, X favors real-time commentary, Reddit favors community helpfulness). One article touching all of them gives maximum distribution surface for minimum marginal effort.
- **CELLO application**: Andre's current content effort (if any) should have a repurposing plan. A CELLO technical blog post becomes: a LinkedIn post about the security architecture, an X thread about what FROST threshold signing means for AI agents, a Reddit comment in the right thread, a short YouTube clip demoing a real session. The platforms that matter most for CELLO's developer audience are X, LinkedIn, GitHub Discussions, and Reddit — not TikTok/Instagram. Prioritizing those four and ignoring the rest is the CELLO-specific version of this tactic.

**[Backlink exchange engine between users in the same niche]**
- **What they did**: Distribute built an automated backlink exchange feature: it finds other Distribute users whose product is in a related niche and arranges mutual backlink exchanges between their websites. Quote: "We will find distributors in your field and exchange backlink with them. This will boost the power of your website."
- **Why they chose it**: Backlinks remain a Google ranking factor. For early-stage SaaS with no domain authority, organic backlink acquisition is slow. A mutual exchange with other SaaS products in adjacent niches is a shortcut — both parties gain. The network effect is real: the more users Distribute has, the richer the backlink exchange pool.
- **CELLO application**: CELLO cannot build an automated exchange engine for itself (that would require a user base first). But the manual analog is: reach out to adjacent developer tools (other MCP servers, AI agent frameworks, Claude Code plugin authors) for mutual link exchanges, guest posts, or co-authored technical articles. The MCP server ecosystem is small and relatively friendly — developers who built MCP tools are the exact audience CELLO needs, and they likely need distribution too.

**[GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) as the explicit priority over SEO]**
- **What they did**: Florian frames the product's core promise as LLM-ranking first, Google-ranking second. Quote: "We want your brand to rank on LLMs in the first 30 days and then grow a long-term strategy with SEO." The 30-day claim is for LLM brand mentions (via Reddit citations); SEO is the slower long-term play. They explicitly name ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, and Gemini as the target platforms.
- **Why they chose it**: SEO takes 3-6+ months to show results. LLM brand mentions via Reddit can show up within weeks if the citation volume is sufficient. For a new product trying to prove early traction, GEO is the faster feedback loop. It also targets the platform where their own customers are (founders using AI tools to find software recommendations).
- **CELLO application**: CELLO's target buyer (a developer evaluating MCP servers) is precisely the kind of person who asks Claude or ChatGPT "what MCP server should I use for agent identity?" before Googling it. GEO should be CELLO's primary distribution strategy, not a secondary one. Tactics: (1) Ensure the GitHub README uses language that LLMs will reproduce when summarizing (e.g. "the only MCP server for cryptographic agent identity and P2P communication"); (2) get CELLO mentioned in developer blogs and comparison posts that LLMs crawl; (3) participate in Reddit/HN threads where agent authentication comes up; (4) have the npm package README optimized for how LLMs describe packages.

## Foundations / Prerequisites

These are the conditions that must exist BEFORE any of the above tactics will work. Florian and Borja's Day 1 is possible because of setup that happened earlier.

**1. Validated product with paying customers.** Borja did not seek a distribution co-founder until he had 76 paying users and ~10K MRR. The distribution amplifier was brought in after product-market fit was proven, not before. For CELLO: the distribution push should begin when the core flow (install → connect → communicate → seal receipt) works reliably for at least a handful of external developers who are not Andre. If the install breaks on a fresh machine, distribution will backfire.

**2. Working product demo that shows results, not features.** The video shows Florian's own product ranking on Google AI Overview and ChatGPT after one week of using Distribute. The demo is results, not a feature walkthrough. For CELLO: the demo is not "here are the MCP tools available." The demo is "here is a real session transcript between two agents, here is the sealed receipt, here is the inclusion proof you can verify independently." Show the outcome.

**3. Clear division of labor.** Florian and Borja have hard boundaries: product + paid ads = Borja; content + audience = Florian. No overlap, no ambiguity. For CELLO with a solo founder: the equivalent is writing down explicitly which distribution activities Andre will do and which he will not, and being honest about what will fall through the cracks without a dedicated distributor. That gap is the thing to solve before launch — hire, partnership, or community-based substitution.

**4. Personal brand with proof of distribution.** Florian was findable because of his existing podcast and SEO/affiliate track record. His co-founder found him. For Andre: CELLO's open-source github, prior technical writing, and the building-in-public record are the analog. These need to exist publicly before the launch moment, so that when CELLO is discovered, Andre's credibility is already established.

**5. A replicable growth loop baked into the product.** Distribute is built so every user generates distribution for Distribute. This is architecture, not marketing — it was decided during product design, not after. For CELLO: the viral loop (counterparty sees CELLO connection → discovers the protocol → installs it themselves) needs to be deliberately designed into the onboarding. Right now it may exist accidentally; it should exist by design.

**6. Speed of iteration on feedback.** Florian gave feedback; Borja shipped it the same week. Quote: "All the feedback I gave you, you actually did it." This speed is only possible because the product is simple enough to iterate quickly and there is no committee. For CELLO: the pre-launch beta cohort's feedback must be actionable within days, not weeks. If the feedback loop is slow, beta testers disengage before the launch.
