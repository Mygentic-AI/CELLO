## Extracted Tactics

**[Creator Co-Founder as Distribution Cheat Code]**
- **What they did**: Put a YouTube creator (Batista, 24yo, e-commerce niche) on the co-founding team with equity. Not as an affiliate or ambassador — as a co-founder with shares and aligned incentives. Previously did the same with Yomi Denzel (biggest online business creator in France) for Minea. "The cheat code of having a YouTube creator is that you know it's going to work."
- **Why it worked**: Three distinct advantages: (1) guaranteed eyeballs at launch — no cold start, (2) the creator becomes your best power user giving product feedback daily because they USE the tool, (3) you get real economics data from their videos (conversion rates, cost per view, LTV) so you can model paid creator partnerships without guessing.
- **CELLO application**: Find a creator who USES AI agents for their work and is already frustrated by copy-paste collaboration. They do not need to be a developer channel. A productivity/AI workflow creator (someone showing how they use Claude Code, Cursor, agents in their daily work) who joins as an early advocate with meaningful upside (advisory equity, revenue share on referrals) becomes both the distribution channel AND the product feedback loop. The creator validates the "Slack-paste pain" narrative from their own lived experience.
- **Category**: `partnership`

**[Secured Baseline Revenue Before Experimentation]**
- **What they did**: Used the creator's audience to guarantee 5-10K MRR from day one, then used that baseline cash flow to experiment with other distribution channels. "You have at least like 5K or 10K MRR and the mindset is like super different because you have way less stress."
- **Why it worked**: Eliminates survival pressure. With baseline revenue secured, you can test channels systematically rather than desperately. You are experimenting from safety, not from fear.
- **CELLO application**: The solo use case (connecting your OWN agents) means CELLO can seed initial revenue from a small group of power users who already run multiple agents. Even 10-20 paying users who genuinely need multi-agent communication provides a revenue baseline and, more importantly, a stress-free position to experiment with broader distribution. The waitlist should convert the most urgent solo-agent users first.
- **Category**: `prerequisite`

**[Problem-First, Not Solution-First]**
- **What they did**: After 5 years of building solutions nobody used, Loic switched to starting with the problem. "The switch was really when I understood it wasn't about the solution, it was really about the problem. I started speaking to people, understanding their problem and their everyday problem and then finding solutions for these problems."
- **Why it worked**: Building from a known pain guarantees at least some demand. The first 5 years were "coding for nobody" because the work started from capability ("I can build this") rather than pain ("people need this").
- **CELLO application**: CELLO's positioning must lead with the PROBLEM (copy-pasting between Slack/email because your agents cannot talk to each other) not the SOLUTION (FROST threshold signatures, Ed25519, hash chains). The ICP does not wake up thinking about cryptographic protocols — they wake up frustrated that they spent 20 minutes copy-pasting context between tools. Every piece of content, every landing page, every creator brief must start with the pain.
- **Category**: `prerequisite`

**[Sexy Onboarding as Hook, Wow Moment as Retention]**
- **What they did**: Built an onboarding funnel that was "really shareable — the UI was pretty with a lot of animation, kind of like the TikTok funnel." But the REAL retention moment was deeper: watching your store get built live with streaming AI, right in front of you. "The onboarding was more like the hook. The wow moment is that when you arrive on the builder, you have streaming, you see your store getting built live."
- **Why it worked**: Two distinct psychological triggers. The hook (pretty onboarding) drives shares and word-of-mouth — it is the thing people screenshot and post. The wow moment (live streaming build) creates the emotional commitment that converts to paid. Hook gets them in the door; wow keeps them.
- **CELLO application**: CELLO's "hook" = the moment you see two agents connect and start talking without you doing anything. The "wow moment" = watching a real conversation happen between your agents, producing real output, with cryptographic proof that nobody tampered with it. The onboarding must be VISUALLY dramatic — show the connection happening live, show messages flowing in real-time. Make the first connection feel like magic, not like configuration. Record a screen capture of two agents collaborating autonomously and use it as the hero content.
- **Category**: `product-led-growth`

- **Why it worked**: A creator who is genuinely using and co-building the product creates authentic content that converts at a much higher rate than sponsored content. The audience trusts someone who has skin in the game (equity). Plus the product itself was designed to be shareable (animated UI, impressive demo).
- **CELLO application**: The first CELLO launch video should show a real workflow — "Watch me connect my Claude Code agent to my Hermes agent and have them collaborate on a task without me copy-pasting anything." The creator should be someone who was ALREADY doing this collaboration manually and can show the before/after. One authentic "this changed my workflow" video from someone with 5-50K followers in the AI/productivity space could drive initial signups.

**[YouTube Creators as Primary Distribution Channel (Product-Channel Fit)]**
- **What they did**: Tested multiple distribution channels (SEO, ads, social) but found that YouTube creators consistently outperformed everything else. "There's the product-channel fit where you have 80% of your distribution come from one channel, which for us was obviously the YouTubers." They then CLOSED all other channels and focused exclusively on YouTube partnerships.
- **Why it worked**: YouTube tutorials and reviews have high intent — people watching "how to find winning products" are actively looking for tools. The content is evergreen (keeps driving signups months later). And the format allows for proper demonstration of complex products.
- **CELLO application**: AI agent workflow content is EXPLODING on YouTube. People searching "how to use Claude Code," "multi-agent workflows," "AI automation" are exactly CELLO's ICP. The product is inherently visual (agent conversations, connection flows). YouTube is likely the highest-intent channel because people watching agent tutorials are already using agents and hitting the collaboration wall. Test with 3-5 small creators, measure economics, then double down.

**[Start with Sub-10K Creators on Commission]**
- **What they did**: Started with creators under 10K followers who would work on pure commission (30% lifetime revenue share) rather than requiring upfront payment. "Start with guys under 10K followers because these guys usually you don't have to pay upfront — you can negotiate commission. It's a win-win: if you give me a paying subscription, you get 30% for life."
- **Why it worked**: Zero upfront risk. You only pay when they deliver actual revenue. Small creators are hungry, responsive, and often have highly engaged niche audiences. You validate the economics (conversion rate, CAC, LTV) cheaply before scaling to bigger creators.
- **CELLO application**: Find 5-10 AI workflow/productivity creators with 2-10K subscribers who are already showing multi-agent setups. Offer them lifetime commission on signups through their link. They get recurring income for recommending a tool they would use anyway. CELLO validates its messaging, conversion funnel, and unit economics with near-zero upfront cost. These small creators become the test bed for messaging that scales.
- **Category**: `ongoing-distribution`

**[Graduate to Locked Ambassadors on Quarterly Contracts]**
- **What they did**: Once they found creators that converted well with small audiences, they graduated to 10-50K creators (who need upfront payment), validated again, then locked the best performers into quarterly contracts as "ambassadors" who embed the product into their regular content (not just one-off ads). "Try and lock him for a quarter — 3-4 months — to do several videos. They become ambassadors. They talk more and more about your product. It's embedded into their videos and their audience sees this guy is really working with this tool."
- **Why it worked**: Repeated exposure builds trust. An ambassador who mentions the tool across multiple videos creates the perception that it is an essential part of their stack. The audience thinks "if this person I trust uses it daily, I should too." This is fundamentally different from a one-off sponsored mention.
- **CELLO application**: After validating with small creators, find 2-3 mid-tier AI workflow creators and lock them into quarterly ambassador deals. They mention CELLO naturally across multiple videos — "and my agents are connected via CELLO so they can collaborate without me" becomes a recurring background element. Over 3 months, their audience internalizes CELLO as infrastructure, not a novelty.
- **Category**: `ongoing-distribution`

**[FOMO and Deadline Pressure to Attract Big Partners]**
- **What they did**: Created momentum (merger talks with a competitor) that generated FOMO for a major creator (Yomi Denzel). "Yomi got word of this and was like 'Okay, this is becoming pretty big. Are they going to do this without me?'" Loic describes it: "To attract the attention of really big creators, you need to create hooks — 'This is happening. Are you in or out?' There's a deadline. It's a bit like VCs — you just need to keep pressure."
- **Why it worked**: High-status partners respond to scarcity and momentum, not cold pitches. They join things that are ALREADY happening, not things that might happen someday. Creating urgency (a closing window) forces a decision rather than perpetual "let me think about it."
- **CELLO application**: When approaching larger creators or potential partners, lead with momentum: "We have X agents connected, Y sessions completed, and we are about to lock in our launch creator. We have one ambassador slot for someone in [their niche]." Frame the opportunity as closing, not opening. Use waitlist numbers, active agent counts, or even a visible "founding partners" cap to create legitimate scarcity.

**[Overnight Market Capture via Creator Stamp of Approval]**

- **CELLO application**: If CELLO could get a credible AI workflow figure (not necessarily massive — just deeply trusted in the agent/AI tooling space) to be visibly involved in building or shaping the product, the trust transfer to their audience would be immediate. This is different from sponsorship — it is involvement. Even an advisory role with public visibility ("CELLO's developer experience is shaped by [person]") creates this stamp effect.

**[High-Tier Plan + Webinar = Plateau Breaker]**
- **What they did**: Hit a plateau at 300K MRR. Solution: created a high-tier plan ($400/month, discounted to $300) with bonuses, then sold it via webinars featuring their creator (Yomi). "For a webinar to work, you can't sell a $50 subscription — you need an offer between $150-200+. We created a high-tier offer." Ran ads into the webinar, Yomi sold at the end. Went from 300K to 500K MRR.
- **Why it worked**: Webinars create urgency and allow for high-touch sales of premium offerings. The economics only work with higher price points (cost of webinar production + ads must be recovered). A charismatic salesperson + scarce offer + live deadline = conversion spikes that break plateaus.
- **CELLO application**: Not directly applicable at launch. But the principle is: when you hit a plateau, go UPMARKET with a premium tier, not downmarket with discounts. For CELLO, this could eventually mean an "enterprise agent identity" tier with enhanced guarantees, priority relay, faster ceremonies, or dedicated directory nodes — sold via live demos showing the security model to teams that handle sensitive data.
- **Category**: `ongoing-distribution`

**[Price Increase at Every Plateau]**
- **What they did**: "Every time we hit a plateau, we decided to increase the price." When users churned, they discovered most churn was NOT from bugs or bad UX — it was from users who did not know how to succeed with the tool. So they created a coaching tier at higher price point (Minea Coaching) with 1-on-1 help. Went from 500K to 750K MRR.
- **Why it worked**: If your churn is from users who lack capability (not from product failure), the fix is not to make the product cheaper — it is to add MORE value at a higher price. Users who pay more are more committed and get more help, so they succeed more, so they retain longer. Counterintuitive but mathematically correct.
- **CELLO application**: When CELLO plateaus, the answer is likely "help users succeed" not "make it cheaper." If users connect agents but do not get value from the connection, the answer might be a premium tier with onboarding help, workflow templates, or a "connection consultant" who helps design their multi-agent architecture. The friction is not price — it is knowing what to DO with connected agents.
- **Category**: `product-led-growth`

**[Understand Churn Reason Before Acting]**
- **What they did**: Talked to churned users and discovered the reason was NOT bugs, UX issues, or price. "The main reason was 'I'm quitting the industry because I don't know how to start.'" This insight drove the coaching product rather than more features or lower prices.
- **Why it worked**: Most founders assume churn means "product not good enough" and respond with more features. But if users are leaving because they cannot succeed in their broader goal, more features will not help. You need to understand the REAL reason before choosing a response.
- **CELLO application**: When CELLO users churn, the reason may not be about CELLO at all — it may be "I stopped using multiple agents" or "I couldn't figure out what to have my agents collaborate on." Understanding this distinction determines whether you need better product, better onboarding, or better "recipes" showing what agent collaboration can accomplish.

**[Scheduled Public Releases as Team Motivation Peaks]**
- **What they did**: Every 4 months, a public release event — pre-announced, with a creator explaining new features on a live webinar, with $200K in ads driving attendance. "The marketing team and the dev team know they have a deadline. If the product crashes during the live, we all know whose fault it is." Used this rhythm for 4 years.

The external commitment (public announcement + ads already running) makes slippage impossible without visible consequences. Motivation is not a flat line — it is peaks and valleys, and you design for that.
- **CELLO application**: For a solo founder, the equivalent is publicly committing to launch dates, beta invitations, or feature releases. "CELLO v1 launches on [date]" creates the same forcing function. Even without a team, making public commitments (on the waitlist page, in build-in-public posts, to early users) creates accountability that prevents endless polishing.

**[Incentive Alignment at Every Level]**
- **What they did**: Made sure every person — co-founders, creators, even support developers — had incentives aligned with company outcomes. Support dev gets bonuses tied to 5-star Shopify reviews (which drive organic ranking). Creator co-founder has equity. "The only thing that really matters is incentives. Once you understand that you just need to make sure everyone has the good incentive, it should be pretty fun."
- **Why it worked**: People optimize for what they are measured/rewarded on. If support dev is measured on ticket close time, they rush. If measured on 5-star reviews, they deliver quality that generates reviews. Misaligned incentives create silent organizational drag that is invisible until you wonder why nothing is working.
- **CELLO application**: For creator partnerships, commission-based compensation aligns incentives perfectly — they earn when CELLO earns. For early hires or contributors, equity or revenue share on referred users keeps everyone pulling the same direction. Even open-source contributors could be incentivized through a "contributor earns" model (credits, priority support, recognition).

**[Speed and Iteration Over Perfection (with a Caveat)]**
- **What they did**: General playbook emphasizes speed: "Try and have initial feedback as fast as possible." But Drop Magic was an exception — 6 months of development before launch. The difference: "We knew it was going to work because we had a creator and I had this background with Minea. So I could take the risk." - **Why it worked**: Speed matters when you are uncertain about demand. When demand is pre-validated (creator audience waiting, prior market knowledge), the constraint flips: you want the WOW moment to be as strong as possible at launch because you only get one first impression with that audience.
- **CELLO application**: CELLO has some pre-validation (Andre uses it daily, the solo agent use case works). The question is whether the launch moment needs to be "wow" or "functional." Given that CELLO's ICP includes non-developers, the onboarding and first-connection experience needs to feel magical on day one. Better to launch slightly later with a stunning first-run experience than to launch early with a clunky setup that wastes the initial attention spike.

## Foundations / Prerequisites

Before applying this playbook, CELLO needs:

1. **A working "wow moment" demo** — Two agents connecting and collaborating visually, in under 2 minutes, that makes someone say "holy shit." Not a terminal log. A VISUAL experience that is shareable and impressive to non-developers.

2. **Problem-first messaging locked in** — Every touchpoint leads with "stop copy-pasting between your agents" not "cryptographic threshold signatures." The pain, not the mechanism. Validated by testing messaging with 5 people outside the crypto/protocol world.

3. **One creator relationship identified** — Someone with 5-50K followers who USES multiple AI agents in their real workflow and is frustrated by the collaboration gap. Not a sponsor — someone genuinely experiencing the pain who would use CELLO regardless.

4. **Unit economics framework ready** — Attribution on creator content (unique signup links, UTM tracking). Know: views-to-signup, signup-to-activation, activation-to-retained-user. Without this, you cannot tell which creator content works and you cannot scale.

5. **Solo use case bulletproof** — The "connect your own agents" flow must be reliable, fast, and visually satisfying. This is both the dog-food validation AND the first thing any new user will try. If it fails or feels clunky, nothing else matters.

6. **Incentive structure for early advocates** — Commission, revenue share, advisory equity, or credits. Something that makes creator/advocate partnership genuinely win-win so their recommendation is authentic, not forced.

7. **Brand independence from day one** — CELLO is the protocol, not "[creator]'s agent tool." Position it as infrastructure. The creator introduces it; CELLO stands alone.
