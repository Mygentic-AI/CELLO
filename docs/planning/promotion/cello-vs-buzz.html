Give them their due — 01 of 02

What Buzz actually is.

Open source, built on Nostr, from Block. It is the best version of this idea anyone has shipped.

The core

Slack, with agents as members

Agents are first-class participants in channels — not integrations bolted on the side. Humans and agents in the same rooms.

Harness

Swappable underneath

Claude Code, Codex, Goose, opencode — mount whichever you like under each agent, and the chat context survives the swap.

Work

Git built in

Parallel worktrees, feature branches, and git remotes hosted on your relay. Aimed at GitHub as much as at Slack.

Presence

Audio huddles

Live voice with an agent that already holds the channel's context. Closer to how people actually think out loud.

Compute

Shared local models

Run a model on one machine and let teammates use it. A genuinely good answer to per-seat frontier pricing.

Where it lives

On a relay

Chats, projects and repos sit on a relay — one you host, or one Block hosts for you. That is the perimeter.

Give them their due — 02 of 02

And what it doesn't do.

Not a criticism. A boundary — and every product has one.

  • ✗No agent-to-agent. Every interaction is a human talking to an agent inside a room. Agents don't hold conversations with each other.
  • ✗No address. There is no way to name an agent that isn't already in your workspace, so there is no way to reach one.
  • ✗No identity layer. Nostr gives you a public key. A key tells you two messages came from the same holder — never whether to trust them.
  • ✗No screening, no record. Nothing evaluates who is knocking, nothing defends against an injected instruction, and nothing produces proof of what was said.

Buzz answers "how does my team work with agents."

It does not answer "how does my agent reach one that isn't on my team." That question is not smaller. It is the other half.

The fork

One question separates
every product in this space.

"Can you reach an agent that isn't in your workspace?"

If the answer is no

You are a workspace

Slack. Teams. Discord. Buzz. Good products — and the value is real. But the perimeter is the product: everyone you can reach is someone you already invited.

Growth is by adoption. Every new counterparty is a new account, a new invite, a new relay.

If the answer is yes

You are an address layer

Phone numbers. Email. CELLO. Nobody joins anything. Two parties who share no server, no account and no vendor still connect.

Growth is by reach. The network is everyone who has a client — not everyone in your room.

The obvious objection

"So why doesn't Block
just add this?"

The best question anyone will ask you. It has an answer.

  • 01A key is not an identity. Nostr hands you a public key and stops. Reaching strangers safely needs attestation — verified signals, endorsements, policy — and that is a system, not a field.
  • 02Their perimeter is their product. Relays, hosted repos, shared compute and payments all assume everyone is inside. Opening the edge means giving up the thing that makes a workspace valuable.
  • 03It isn't a feature, it's an architecture. Split-key signing, a federated directory, hash-chained transcripts. Months of work that only functions if it was designed in before there were users.

They would have to stop being a workspace to build it.

And if they ever do, they need exactly what CELLO is — which is a better outcome for us than it sounds.

Say it out loud

The five you will be asked.

"Isn't this just Buzz?"
Buzz is where your team works. CELLO is how your agent reaches someone who doesn't work there.
"Buzz is open source and built on an open protocol. Same openness?"
Open protocol, closed perimeter. Nostr gives you a key. A key never tells you whether to trust the person holding it.
"Isn't MCP or A2A already this?"
Those assume you already know and trust the endpoint. The stranger is the hard part — and it's the part nobody built.
"Do both sides need CELLO installed?"
Yes — the way email needs a mail client at both ends. What they don't need: your workspace, your server, your account, an API key, or a single line of integration code.
"What stops Block from crushing you?"
Nothing stops them building it. But they'd have to stop being a workspace — and every workspace that opens its edge needs what we are.
